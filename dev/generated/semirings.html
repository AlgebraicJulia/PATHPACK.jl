One Graph, Many Questions ​

Download notebookThe same code as on this page, ready to run in Jupyter with IJulia.

The star of a matrix depends on the semiring it is computed in. On one graph with one set of weights, a different semiring answers a different question, and PATHPACK.jl computes each one with the same call and the same solver.

Below, one landscape: north-central Florida as a grid of 67,411 land-cover cells, each step weighted by how hard that ground is for a forest animal to cross. Three semirings give three different maps, each the row of one forest cell in the all-pairs answer.

mstar(MinPlus(), land)

A Small Landscape ​

A 30 × 30 grid stands in for the map: each cell has a resistance between 1 (open forest) and 20 (a road or a town), and a step between neighbours costs the mean of the two resistances.

julia
using PATHPACK, SparseArrays, Random
using PATHPACK.CPU: MinPlus, MinMax, MaxMin, PlusProd, mstar

Random.seed!(2)
k = 30; n = k * k
id(i, j) = (j - 1) * k + i
r = rand([1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 2.0, 5.0, 20.0], k, k)
I = Int[]; J = Int[]; C = Float64[]
for j in 1:k, i in 1:k
    for (di, dj) in ((1, 0), (0, 1))
        (i + di <= k && j + dj <= k) || continue
        c = (r[i, j] + r[i + di, j + dj]) / 2
        a, b = id(i, j), id(i + di, j + dj)
        push!(I, a, b); push!(J, b, a); push!(C, c, c)
    end
end
A = sparse(I, J, C, n, n)
s, t = id(1, 1), id(k, k);    # from one corner to the other

The Cheapest Route: MinPlus ​

With ⊕ = min and ⊗ = +, a path's weight is its total cost and the star keeps the cheapest: the least-cost distance of landscape ecology.

julia
D = mstar(MinPlus(), A)
D[s, t]
76.5

The Worst Barrier: MinMax ​

With ⊕ = min and ⊗ = max, a path's weight is its single worst step, and the star keeps the path whose worst step is mildest. It tells whether two places are connected without ever crossing a step above some cost: the barriers that split a landscape.

julia
B = mstar(MinMax(), A)
B[s, t]
3.0

The Widest Route: MaxMin ​

Flip both operations and the weights become capacities: with ⊕ = max and ⊗ = min the star is the most capacity a single route can carry, as for the bandwidth of a network or the heaviest truck a road network allows. Here, with ease of crossing (one over the cost) as the capacity:

julia
E = copy(A); nonzeros(E) .= 1 ./ nonzeros(E)
W = mstar(MaxMin(), E)
W[s, t] == 1 / B[s, t]    # the widest route in ease is the mildest route in cost
true

Where a Wandering Animal Goes: PlusProd ​

With ⊕ = + and ⊗ = ×, the star sums over every walk instead of picking one. Take the random walk that steps to a neighbour with probability proportional to how easy it is to cross, and survives each step with probability β: the star (I - βP)⁻¹ holds the expected number of visits to every cell from every start.

julia
β = 0.999
P = spdiagm(β ./ vec(sum(E; dims = 2))) * E
V = mstar(PlusProd(), P)
V[s, t], V[s, s]
(0.19474015154394841, 5.4437790590935835)

The same sum over walks, with other weights, gives exact personalized PageRank for graph neural networks and walk counts in a network.

Every Semiring ​

Semiring⊕, ⊗The star answers
MinPlusmin, +shortest paths
MaxPlusmax, +longest paths (on graphs without positive cycles)
MaxMinmax, minwidest paths
MinMaxmin, maxbottleneck paths
MaxProdmax, ×most reliable paths, with probabilities as weights
PlusProd+, ×sums over all walks
OrAnd∪, ∩ on sets of bitsreachability

All of them are exported by PATHPACK.CPU; the GPU solver takes a semiring through apsp_gpu(A; semiring = ...).
